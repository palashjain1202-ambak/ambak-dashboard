## 3. Key Discussion Points

- **Meta-to-Google budget shift:** Google CPA is Rs 400 vs Meta Rs 100, but Google delivers pull customers (higher ticket size, better intent). The shift helped QL quality. Team has matured post-March hiring. SME program also contributed. Lead volume is lower but quality is higher.

- **Cost per login trend:** Currently Rs 8,500, down to Rs 6,500 in April week 3/4. Improvement tracks directly with lead-to-login conversion going up. May expected to sustain or improve further.

- **M0 sanction problem (critical):** M0 sanction rate is 13%, described as "pathetic." Historical correlation is clear - when M0 is below 20%, total login-to-sanction does not cross 30%. April ended at 38% sanction, dragged by the M0 dip. Raghuveer showed the data clearly: Feb's 22% M0 drove 39% total sanction.

- **Partial login root cause debate:** 30% of the bucket remains partial or semi-complete login at any point, taking 15-20 days to move. Rashi and Ankit debated causality. Raghuveer rejected partial login as an excuse - the team itself created those partial logins. Pushed for proactive mid-month customer outreach (call customers on Sundays, partner with sales ops). SME team now downloading partial cases from last week.

- **QL performance:** 621 vs 727 target (85% achievement on count). Amount target achieved or exceeded. Per-RM QL rate is ~9.5, with 3-to-6-month cohort performing best (though small at only 8 people). 6-month+ cohort still at 14-17 QLs vs 20+ target.

- **Delhi NCR gap:** 64 vs 136 QL target (47%). Three root causes identified: (a) Noida has no FBM - two experienced RMs (Shivam and Shubham) doing logins themselves, coordination between RMs and Ankit's FBMs failing; (b) most of Delhi team is 0-3 months tenure; (c) onboarding-to-activation rate is low. Three-point June plan agreed: FBM-RM coordination fix for Noida, hiring review with Pooja, 3-day partner activation sprint.

- **Pune persistent failure (critical):** 6+ months of misses. On May 25, visibility was Rs 13.5 CR. Actual disbursal: Rs 6.2 CR. This pattern has repeated every month. Raghuveer challenged the team sharply - local team blamed, national call center piloted as replacement, but both have now missed. Pushed for cross-team ownership (Rashi, Rishab, Ankit, Ishant, Vikas, Shalesh) to sit together and create a ground-up solution rather than sequential experiments.

- **Unassigned QLs issue:** 45 QLs remain unassigned out of 621. Only Rs 88 lakh disbursed from Delhi's unassigned bucket, Rs 4.32 CR from Lucknow. Raghuveer challenged the team on why unassigned leads are counted in QL targets if conversion is near zero.

- **CAM structure (Abdullah / Devesh):** Raghuveer pushed for a full system: onsite/offsite resource SLAs backed by Pooja and Arpit, a standardised client report format designed by Pranav, quarterly NPS collection from clients, and a product-tech value proposition for CAM accounts. Weekly CAM cadence call proposed (Abdullah + Ankit, expanded group).

- **E2E disbursement classification:** May E2E disbursement is Rs 62 CR (with 6 CR confirmation pending from Naveen). Within this: Rs 25 CR high margin (not pure E2E), Rs ~37 CR from Ankit's team (pure E2E, Rs 15 CR) plus self. ## 7. Risks / Things to Watch

- **M0 sanction rate at 13% is structurally broken:** Every prior month where M0 was this low, total login-to-sanction ended at 28-30%. June will repeat unless partial login pipeline is aggressively worked down mid-month, not at month end.

- **Pune has now missed for 6+ consecutive months and no solution has held:** Each month a new explanation or initiative is offered (local team, national call center, Delhi team takeover). The risk is that June becomes month seven with the same pattern.

- **Unassigned QL inflation:** 45 unassigned leads with near-zero conversion being counted in QL targets inflates the QL number and understates the true gap. If sales RMs are using unassigned leads as a morale buffer and nothing changes, this will mask true capacity.

- **Delhi volume stagnation:** QL count went from 67 to 64 month-on-month despite new hiring. If 70% of the team is under 3 months tenure and 3-to-6-month performers have already been let go, Delhi may not recover in time for the steep June target of 136+.

- **June disbursal target of Rs 250 CR is ~90 CR above May's likely outcome:** The gap is very large. With Pune unreliable, Delhi lagging, and no confirmed Mahada leads until June 15, the path to Rs 250 CR depends on multiple moving parts landing simultaneously.

- **E2E vs high margin classification is unresolved in investor reporting until product tray is built (June second week):** Any investor conversation before that risks presenting inflated pure E2E numbers.

- **Insurance payout process for partners is not streamlined:** Partners who contributed leads in Jan-Mar dropped off, likely due to unclear or delayed payout. Without fixing this before scaling to Rs 60 lakh June target, the same partners may churn again.

- **LAP retention was problematic in April due to JFM payout mismanagement:** Corrective steps were taken by May 17. If those corrections do not reflect in May's net retention numbers, the issue may compound into June.

## 8. Meeting Dynamics and Blind Spots

- **Raghuveer carried almost all the analytical weight.** He was the one reading the charts, identifying correlations (M0 vs total sanction, unassigned QL conversion), and drawing conclusions. The functional leads mostly responded reactively. A healthier dynamic would have them surfacing these patterns proactively before month end.

- **"We started this last week" was the default defence across multiple issues:** Partial login SME assignment, ITC task creation, call patches to RMs - in every case the team had just begun acting. The pattern of starting initiatives in the final week of the month is recurring and was explicitly called out by Raghuveer. No one challenged this dynamic or proposed a structural fix.

- **Pune has had no single named owner for six months.** Multiple people talked about it (Ankit, Rashi, Vikas, Rishab, Shalesh were all mentioned), but no one said "I own this." Raghuveer asked "who is taking charge" directly. The answer was diffuse. This is the clearest blind spot in the meeting.

- **Avinash and Abdullah were often asked to defend their numbers mid-flow with limited data at hand.** This created confusion and slowed the discussion. Monthly reviews should not be the first time pod owners are asked to reconcile city-level discrepancies.

- **Rashi's contributions were limited to a few comments.** Given her role in ops and distribution, she had less airtime than expected on the partial login issue, the Pune accountability question, and city-level execution planning. The ops perspective was largely filtered through Ankit.

- **The insurance discussion was positive but shallow.** Vijay and Tarun have a Rs 60 lakh June target with no clear partner pipeline visibility and a known payout process problem. This received relatively brief scrutiny compared to the E2E numbers.
