# CoS Report — Resume Storytelling Framework for Placements — 2026-05-19

## 1. Context

A campus placement training session run for student attendees, focused on how to write resumes that read well to recruiters. The speaker frames the session around storytelling, emotional detachment from past work, and bullet-writing fundamentals, and uses placecom members' resumes as live examples. Attendance counts toward a mandatory CPP requirement but does not guarantee shortlisting.

- Unknown A (host) – Placement coach / facilitator. Focus: resume structure, recruiter-lens framing, do's and don'ts walkthroughs.
- Unknown B (placecom members) – Student volunteers from the placement committee. Focus: providing their resumes as in-session examples.
- Unknown C (student participants) – Job-seeking students attending for CPP credit and resume guidance. Focus: applying to consulting, FMCG, HR, finance, and similar roles.

## 3. Key Discussion Points

- Unknown A opens with logistics: camera off, asks students to take extensive notes and use chat for questions.
- Unknown A flags that attendance fulfils the mandatory CPP requirement but does not guarantee shortlisting.
- Unknown A lays out the session structure: objective, resume sections and frameworks, examples, then live critique of placecom resumes.
- Unknown A argues that good resume principles are sector-agnostic across consulting, FMCG, HR, and finance.
- Unknown A introduces the core thesis that a resume must tell a story of why the candidate fits the role.
- Unknown A pushes students to be emotionally detached from past projects and positions when writing bullets.
- Unknown A frames every bullet as something the recruiter must be able to "make sense of," not just a description of activity.
- Unknown A uses a dashboard example to contrast weak ("I built a dashboard") versus strong ("why and what happened") bullet writing.
- Unknown B (placecom) agreed in advance to let their resumes be used for the group walkthrough later in the session.
- Unknown C raised a clarifying question in chat about how to define a "perfect role as a human," which Unknown A deferred to the session itself.

## 7. Risks / Things to Watch

- Students may treat CPP attendance as sufficient and skip the resume rework, missing the actual shortlisting lever.
- Tech hiccups early in the session (camera off, mouse issues, rejoining) cost time and may compress the live walkthrough.
- Emotional attachment to past projects is the main bias the session is trying to break; students may resist editing bullets they personally value.
- Using real placecom resumes as examples could cause discomfort or feedback that lands personally rather than instructionally.
- The "sector-agnostic" framing may under-serve students applying to specialised roles (e.g., quant finance, technical PM) that expect different bullet conventions.
- No written deck is being shared, only a live notes doc, so post-session reference material for students will be thin unless the recording or notes are circulated.
