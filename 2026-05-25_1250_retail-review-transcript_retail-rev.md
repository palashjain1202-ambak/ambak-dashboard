## TL;DR

April retail NR fell ~42 lakhs short of plan: ICICI Bank's pay-in collapsed from 96 to 90 bps on its highest-ever volume month (102 crore), payouts were not cut in step, and at least three lenders (True Home Lab, Bajaj Housing Gross ICE, ICICI) had category and payout decisions made without full slab knowledge. The structural fix - payout autonomy now sitting firmly with the business team - has been decided, but the bigger unresolved watch item is building the 200 crore/month E2E throughput required for the July-October AOP.

## 3. Key Discussion Points

- **Region P&L waterfall:** Bangalore and Gujarat were deficit on the top line versus AOP. NCR had a good volume month but suffered pay-in dilution via ICICI. Bombay was the most challenging region despite a 42% LAP mix - missed LAP pay-in plan by 10 bps and had a very high self payout of 99 bps vs 93 planned. (Vikas, Raghu)

- **HL and LAP pay-in dilution numbers:** HL pay-in landed at 92 against 98 planned. LAP pay-in was 131 against 135, despite LAP mix rising to 31% vs 27% assumed in AOP. The gap is explained by specific lender events, not a general market movement. (Vikas)

- **ICICI Bank pay-in collapse:** ICICI did the highest volume (~102 crore) but average pay-in fell from JFM levels of ~96 bps to 90 bps post the 80-20 change. The business had increased payouts to ICICI to capture market share during JFM but did not reduce them when pay-in dropped, causing a structural NR drag especially in NCR. (Vikas, Vijay, Raghu)

- **Aditya Birla Housing payout cap:** A ~13 crore LAP case hit an ABHFL payout cap, delivering only 96 paise pay-in - but Ambak paid out 95% on it, resulting in near-zero NR retention on a large case. Danish (lender contact) is reportedly following up on whether the cap was waived. (Vikas)

- **Specific category error examples (Raghu pressed for these):** (1) True Home Lab - put in Category 1 without checking if pay-in slabs were achievable; team learned after analysis they should have paid out at only 90% of pay-in since ABHFL slabs were not hit. (2) Bajaj Housing Gross ICE - categorised without understanding the sub-slab and B2B/B2C distinction; exact cause still unclear internally. (3) ICICI - target-linked pay-in (135 then 125 on hitting target) was not communicated to the business team by Aman; payouts were set against the old 135 figure. (Vijay, Aman, Raghu)

- **Decision rights resolved cleanly:** Raghu ruled: payout autonomy and NR ownership sit with the retail business team; any pay-in change is a utility input to that team; the he-said-she-said between Aman and the business team ends here. A 27-28 May sync was proposed to align for May. (Raghu)

- **Universal scheme rationalisation:** Three slabs expanded to six, effective June (finalized ~10th May; existing May scheme runs this month). More slabs give the business team finer payout control by volume tier. (Vikas)

- **Special-scheme quota system:** Launched from May. Previously lead-linked (any case could get a special); now value-linked with a personal quota per RM capped by parameters. This should structurally reduce unmanaged special deal leakage. (Vikas)

- **Special-scheme partner quality (Adi's analysis):** 79.8% of special deals went to sub-2-crore partners that are likely value destroyers. On aggregate, NR from special schemes equals universal schemes because large back-to-back deals offset the small-partner drain - but removing the small-partner leakage via quotas should release NR. (Adi, Raghu)

- **NCR HL/LAP structural drag:** Even at 5-6% of ICICI market, NCR HL and LAP have 8.2 bps NR retention on 443 crore volume. Raghu asked how to solve it. Vikas pointed to only two levers: back-to-back pay-in negotiation at scale + rationalising payouts where market confidence exists. Described as "invasive work." (Raghu, Vikas)

- **Three monthly P&L closes proposed:** Raghu proposed running three closes per month: (1) first-of-month directional view, (2) ~95%-confidence view around the 5th-7th, (3) official mid-month close on the 16th. This halves the action lag from the current two-month cycle. Depends on Pankaj proxying out-of-system business first. (Raghu)

- **Out-of-system business problem:** Roughly half of retail business is out of system. Pankaj to proxy it with estimated closure dates and discount factors (e.g. start at 80% then calibrate down), so that pay-in can be calculated on a directional basis. (Raghu, Pankaj)

- **E2E pay-in positive surprise and Jul-Oct AOP gap:** E2E April pay-in was 109 bps vs 91 AOP - a positive surprise due to higher private lender mix. But May E2E is tracking ~65-70 crore, and the July-October AOP requires ~200 crore/month. This is the single biggest capacity gap to build now. (Vikas)

- **NR waterfall summary:** Total NR delta was -42 lakhs vs plan. Self contributed -26 lakhs (2 bps pay-in dilution + 3 bps payout = 5 bps total), E2E and insurance were primarily volume variances (positive and negative respectively). (Adi)

- **Headcount and productivity:** Productivity dashboard sent to HR and Vijay. 19 flagged; 16 moving to exit or performance conversations. Rating structure is vintage-wise with different weightages for 0-3 months, 3-6 months, and 6+ months. Ashish raised whether 12+ month RMs should have NR carry a higher weightage. (Vikas, Ashish)

- **ABB Calculator - strong product, zero enablement:** Raghu flagged the ABB calculator as phenomenal based on eight meetings the day before. Partners were seeing it for the first time. Zero partner-facing training material exists. Nitin to build enablement immediately. (Raghu)

- **Marketing/shankars:** Only 3 shankars in April (Noida, Brijana, Delhi) with 19 partners, 3 logins, 1 crore in logins. Nitin presented distribution automation tool (WhatsApp channel scheduling, segment-specific creatives). Raghu's idea: join existing BD WhatsApp groups with a system-driven bot that reads group queries and responds with Ambak collateral (the "periscope" system Pankaj confirmed exists). (Nitin, Raghu)

- **QR-code partner collateral:** Raghu proposed printing partner-specific QR-code collateral for brokers and builders - QR maps to the partner's ID in the system so sourced customers are attributed directly. Pankaj confirmed the system exists. Stickers/printouts for broker desks suggested. (Raghu, Pankaj)

Targets: Vriddhi (ex-Bajaj CEO connected), Adara (facing growth challenges - potentially motivated), Shubham. (Arshay, Raghu)

## 4. Decisions Made

- Payout autonomy and NR ownership stay with the retail business team; pay-in structure and Aman's role unchanged. Owner: Vikas.
- Universal scheme rationalised from 3 to 6 slabs, effective June. Owner: Vikas.
- Special-scheme quota system implemented from May (value-linked, not lead-linked). Owner: Vikas.
- 16 of 19 flagged underperformers move into exit and performance conversations. Owner: Vikas with HR.
- Adopt three monthly P&L closes: first-of-month, 95%-confidence, and mid-month 16th. Owner: Vikas and Adi.
- Retail training team to be rebranded Business Enablement. Owner: Arpit.

## 7. Risks / Things to Watch

- April errors were surfaced only on the 20th-21st of May. Fixes started in March pay off in June at the earliest. May is partly exposed with limited levers remaining.
- Roughly half of retail business is out of system, which makes both the three-close process and real-time pay-in tracking impossible until Pankaj resolves this.
- The July-October AOP of ~200 crore/month E2E throughput is a large, unbuilt capacity gap with no current build plan articulated.
- Pay-in and payout misalignment is structurally recurring (ICICI, Aditya Birla, True Home Lab, Bajaj Gross ICE). Enforcement of the new structure still depends on human discipline, not system controls.
- 79.8% of special deals concentrated in sub-2-crore value-destroying partners. Quota system may face resistance or informal workarounds from sales.
- ABB calculator shipped with zero partner-facing enablement despite being a strong product - an existing growth lever that is idle.
- Training is committed across retail, institutional, and D2C simultaneously for six months. The Business Enablement team is stretched and content quality risk is real if business sign-off is slow.

## 8. Meeting Dynamics and Blind Spots

- **Raghu was the sole probing engine.** Every substantive question, every challenge, every redirect - including surfacing the ABB calculator gap, the QR-code idea, and the WhatsApp periscope suggestion - came from Raghu. No other leader opened a productive thread.

- **The Aman/business-team accountability dispute ran as a he-said-she-said until Raghu intervened.** Two years into the business, the boundary between pay-in information and payout decision-making had not been settled between these teams. It required the CEO to adjudicate.

- **Vijay conceded learning pay-in slabs only after the fact** ("I got it later") without this being treated as a serious accountability failure. The room softened it into "closer tracking." The underlying norm - that BD can approve categories without verifying slabs - was not challenged.

- **Marketing presented activity metrics throughout, not outcome metrics.** Nitin's deck led with shankar counts, WhatsApp follower growth, and creative output. Raghu redirected to partner activation, retention, and revenue impact every single time. The ABB calculator - Raghu's strongest endorsement of the entire session - had zero marketing support, and this was not self-identified by Nitin.

- **Key distribution ideas (QR codes, WhatsApp periscope, ABB enablement) originated from Raghu, not from marketing or sales.** These are core distribution tactics. The function that owns them should be bringing them, not the CEO.

- **Training presented confidently but without any before/after evidence of RM capability.** The 93% assessment score is internally set. Raghu asked whether new joiners could actually pitch to a partner or customer - Arpit deferred rather than answering. The quality bar is self-referential.

- **Several items landed without owners or dates** until Raghu explicitly asked for them. The default pattern is: Raghu proposes idea, room agrees, no one writes their name on it.
