## 3. Key Discussion Points

- **Training consistency and scheduling gaps** - Speakers including Akash, Devesh, Arpit, and Pooja discussed that the training schedule frequently stretches from 12 to 16 days due to trainer unavailability or travel. Arpit has laid a collaborative framework with institutional heads (Vishal, Taj, Akash) running city-specific content. Raghu asked Pooja to double-click on three areas: (1) train-the-trainer infrastructure and content quality, (2) scheduling fixes, and (3) assessment reliability.

- **QL targets and weekly tracking** - Ishant is targeting 190 QLs this week to reach 680-700 MTD, planning 800 next month via headcount expansion. Raghu pushed for data: are all weeks consistent (Ishant says yes, data-driven) and asked for last 12 weeks of QL numbers. Institutional QL MTD is at 79% (404 leads), retail QL at 40%.

- **Login gaps by city** - Logins across weeks: 84, 82, and declining. Bangalore has ~7-8 files ready but customer consent and property document pendency is delaying login. Raghu warned this has historically not caught up the following week either. Ankit and Akash confirmed Pune logins have improved with document collection push but QL marking (refundable/non-refundable token) remains a coordination friction point between Pune and Delhi.

- **Delhi login stagnation and retail pipeline gap** - Delhi retail QL achievement at 34%; visibility of 6.3 CR against a 21.9 CR target. Mumbai retail shows 10 CR visibility. Total retail visibility across Ankit's team is approximately 16-18 CR. The gap between what Ankit sees and what Vijay's team tracks is a recurring reconciliation problem.

- **Raghu's DSA-level feedback to Ankit** - Raghu stated that Ambak pays 10-15% above market with the most aggressive incentives in the market, yet 3-4 people in the DSA channel are doing 50 CR with far less. His direct assessment: "efforts are kam lag rahi hain, understanding is weak." He challenged Ankit to understand bank-wise trends, build hacky checklists and suggestion matrices, and know at what stage to give up on a case or escalate to a banker. Ankit acknowledged and committed to sharing timelines by the next day.

- **Disbursement target confidence - institutional** - Delhi institutional: 19.6 CR with 100% confidence from Deepak. Mumbai institutional: Puneet confident of 31+ CR minimum (took a 40 CR target). Overall institutional projection: ~90-100 CR best case. Raghu noted he has never seen a month where projection-to-actual does not slip by 15-25% and pushed for realistic assessment.

- **Ahmedabad performance - persistent underperformance** - Neerav has 8 RMs, offers out to 6 more for June joining, and ABM finalization committed by this week. Both Bangalore and Ahmedabad ran fully red P&L in April. Raghu noted Ahmedabad specifically has been a recurring agenda item with no team stability resolution. He pointed out that Basic Home Loans does 300-400 CR a month in Bangalore alone - the market is not the constraint.

- **City scaling ambition vs current gaps** - Monthly growth targets for May-August: 26%, 26%, 40%, 17%. October AOP: NCR 1225 CR, Naveen (North) 1225 CR, Danish (Gujarat) 286 CR, Neerav (Ahmedabad) 85 CR. Raghu made explicit that each city this year must carry its own weight - last year one city could compensate for another; that model no longer applies.

- **E2E pipeline reconciliation** - Pan-India MTD: 473 CR vs 324 CR at same period last month. E2E target is 76 CR. Achieved as of call: 22.82 CR in-system. Vijay's team has a 55+ CR pipeline; Ankit tracks 16.4 CR. The gap: sourcing vs fulfillment attribution creates double-counting confusion (Delhi-sourced cases showing up in Mumbai). Rashi led a case-by-case DID review post-Raghu-drop, identifying sanctioned cases, PDD pendencies, and blank-update gaps across 84 cases.

- **MAHADA leads (2600 leads, 1.2 CR data)** - Expected delivery from Hada in the first week of June. Ankit confirmed tracker created, scripts and campaigns being trained. Raghu pushed for dedicated review separate from the main pipeline: "2600 is an insanely large number - approach it with branding, communication, PD and operational bandwidth." Ankit acknowledged bandwidth constraints; Raghu pushed back firmly that no bandwidth excuses are acceptable here.

- **Insurance data not on call** - Tarun and Hitesh were not initially added to the call. Raghu asked Vijay to get Tarun to send an insurance waterfall update (issued, pipeline, login, lead stage) by afternoon as Raghu has an investor meeting at 2 PM.

But the response pattern (partial acknowledgment + reference to what has already been done) is a mild deflection; watch whether the timelines delivered tomorrow reflect genuine depth or another round of process updates.
- **Neerav gave consistent reassurance without structural solutions** - each time Raghu challenged Ahmedabad, Neerav responded that he is "aware" and "working on it." The ABM hiring has been discussed for at least two cycles now; there was no post-mortem on why it has not closed.

- **The MAHADA lead opportunity (2600 leads) received fewer than 5 minutes of attention** despite Raghu flagging it as "insanely large." Bandwidth concerns were raised without being challenged or resolved. A 2600-lead batch arriving in one week deserves its own planning session, not a passing mention.
- **City heads other than Akash were largely passive** - Danish, Shailesh, Nagesh, Deepak, and Puneet each spoke for under 2 minutes and primarily to confirm targets. No city head raised a structural concern, a resource request, or a new idea.
