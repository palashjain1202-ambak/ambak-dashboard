## 3. Key Discussion Points

- **April institutional P&L metrics:** EPS was 55 lakhs, payout volume 30 lakhs, average payout 50 points. Incentives were directly correlated with RMC payouts. JFM disbursed 200+ crore but only ~1.8 lakhs of incentive was paid out. Insurance attachment 27%; 2.7-2.8 lakhs of premium closed in April, with more in the pipeline. (Ankit, Raghu)

- **Net revenue not moving despite QLs on track:** Raghu made this the central question of the meeting. Three cited reasons: high payouts relative to pay-in, financial NBA (incentive scheme mechanics), and weak QL-to-login conversion. Raghu repeatedly noted that if QLs are on track but NR is flat, the waterfall is invisible and leadership cannot manage. (Raghu, Ankit)

- **E2E QL-to-login collapse:** April QL-to-login conversion fell to ~25% from a historic 40%. This directly hit M0 disbursals. May logins recovering - 172 logins already by mid-May vs 170 for all of April. Target for May is 250-plus logins and the 110 disbursal number. (Ankit, Raghu)

- **Payout as % of disbursement concern:** Payout volume at 73-77%+ of cases was above projection; DTI contribution was well below plan. Incentives are provisioned at ~3 lakhs for the month. Payouts are linked to pay-in but some are flat payouts by partner first-name scheme. (Ankit)

- **Headcount significantly below plan:** 61 people against an AOP expectation of 77, with heavy 0-3 month churn. End-May target is 82. About 70 people exited in April (performance and voluntary), 25 were added. 3-6 month cohort productivity jumped from 7 to 15, primarily because of the December-January intake improving. (Ankit)

- **Productivity metrics:** 7.6 per hour vs target 8. 0-3 month cohort low due to recent hires not yet performing; 3-6 month cohort is the standout - improving to 15. New people added April 13-20 will contribute from May. (Ankit)

- **66% repeat-partner QL figure:** 66% of April QLs came from repeat partners (gave a QL in March too). Cross-share at ~24%. Raghu asked Akash to double-check this figure as it seemed high. Repeat partners in absolute terms have been flat at 52-56 across Jan-April even as active partners grew from 233 to 245. This is the retention quality gap. (Ankit, Raghu)

- **Onboarding trend:** Jan 113, Feb 171, March 208, April 261 - consistently growing. Onboarding-to-activation time dropped to 7-7.8 days, ~80% reduction since start of year. But repeat partners flat despite active-partner growth - Raghu identified this as the core retention problem to solve. (Ankit, Raghu)

- **One-team operating model (core structural directive):** Raghu stated plainly that institutional and E2E have been operating as two separate teams for 1.5 years, presenting competing narratives. Ishaan presents one story, Ankit another. Neither is wrong, but there is no single business story. The fix is a joint first-of-month review. Raghu referenced his experience at PharmEasy where different team leaders ran sub-reviews but all contributed to one unified business review. (Raghu)

- **Ishaan rated the meeting 2/10 for insight:** Unprompted, Ishaan said he learned more from the finance review than from this meeting. Raghu took this constructively and proposed sitting together with Rishabh to redesign what the institutional review should actually drive. (Ishaan, Raghu)

- **WBR/MBR restructuring:** Raghu proposed reversing the MBR flow - lead with P&L and the waterfall of drivers (hits, misses, what worked, what didn't), then discuss initiatives. Current flow leads with data and then unstructured insights. The joint first-of-month review to be the new anchor point. (Raghu)

- **CAMP / large-partner accounts:** CAMP covers Thinker, Aspect, QACR, and Suarez (a travel partner from Ubud). Proptiger is now splitting wallet share with one competitor (Bipartomoney); Ishaan expects to eliminate that competitor this month. Delhi's Amano Technology open house in April resulted in no sales - first time this has happened. Large partners are mostly builders across Mumbai, Pune, Nandhi, and Hyderabad. (Ankit)

- **CAM roles-and-responsibilities framework:** Proposed structure: builders owned by CAM, homebuyers owned by Ops, joint Cam-Ops governance for large accounts. A partner fortnightly econ proposed. CRM to be set up with a requisition form for large-partner tagging. Framework was approved in principle; owner, lead, and cadence to be defined. (CAM/Ops Lead, Raghu)

- **Product roadmap (four items):** (1) One-click dialer - testing now, reduces manual touchpoints. (2) Website partner notification and tagging - now live; partner channel is now tagged separately, post-onboarding RM scheduling enabled. (3) Spot calling and queue logic. (4) Vertical sales app for online apps - Ishaan and Prakash gathering requirements; targets 60%+ lead-to-visit conversion, 10% visit-to-closure. (Ankit, Ishaan)

- **Institutional marketing presentation:** RM event run; playbook ready; 12 high-impact stories collected for amplification; LinkedIn plan for large B2B partnerships drafted. Raghu pushed back: asked for the 2-month business lift in logins and disbursals after RM events before amplifying further. Marketing presented enthusiastically on output; Raghu redirected to outcome measurement. (Institutional Marketing Lead, Raghu)

- **Government drive:** Target is 110 disbursals vs 90; board members are now going directly after key partners. At 38-40 currently; May commitment is 110. (Ankit)

- **Training (Arvind):** HAPA framework training completed; feedback score 3.74/4; 81% attendance on refresh sessions; 6 people missed for unexplained reasons. LST revamp is 70% complete - going live next month after closing with Vishal and Ishaan. LPL quizzes launching weekly. First-time manager program open on nomination basis. Raghu's direct question: will new joiners after this training be able to pitch confidently to a customer or partner? Arvind deferred to Ishaan/Vishal. (Arvind, Raghu)

- **Raghu-Arvind catchup scheduled for next Wednesday:** Full update on D2C, ops, institutional and retail to be covered in about an hour. (Raghu)

## 7. Risks / Things to Watch

- Institutional and E2E have been separate teams for 1.5 years; the missing single business story is the most consequential unresolved structural issue.
- Net revenue is stuck around 30-40% retention with no visible waterfall - leadership cannot attribute the miss or set priorities.
- High payouts continue to erode NR; incentives are linked to payout, creating a circular reinforcement.
- QL-to-login conversion collapsed from 40% to 25%, directly impacting M0 disbursals - the RCA and fix are still outstanding.
- Headcount is well short of plan (61 vs 77) with heavy early-tenure churn - the May target of 82 requires adding 21 people by month end.
- Repeat partners stagnant (52-56) despite active-partner growth of 25% - signals a retention quality problem that will compound.
- Large-partner and CAMP accounts are not yet structured in a CRM, making accountability for large-account progress fragile.

## 8. Meeting Dynamics and Blind Spots

- **Raghu carried nearly all the probing and the push for structural change.** Ishaan and Ankit were largely reactive; no functional lead opened a structural question.

- **Ishaan's 2/10 rating was the most direct piece of self-awareness in the meeting** - he said he got more from the finance review. This candid signal that the review format is broken was acknowledged but not resolved in-room. No one designed the new format on the spot; it was deferred to a future restructure.

- **The institutional-vs-E2E divide kept surfacing as competing narratives.** The framing of "Ishaan tells one story, Ankit tells another" was acknowledged by both. But the resolution - a joint first-of-month review - was agreed in principle without a firm transition plan, clear owner, or format.

- **The joint review and MBR restructure were agreed without dates or single ownership.** "First of the month" was the only anchor. Who owns the agenda, who chairs, what format - all deferred.

- **Marketing presented impact stories enthusiastically.** Raghu had to redirect from story count to business lift measurement. The marketing function appears to be optimising for content volume rather than business output - the same pattern observed in the retail review.

- **Several large-partner and CAM items were discussed at length and parked with ownership left open.** The CAM framework was approved in principle but the question of who owns it, leads it, and at what cadence was explicitly deferred.

- **Training was not stress-tested on real capability.** Raghu asked whether new joiners can actually pitch after training - Arvind deferred. No one offered a mock-call result, a before/after comparison, or a partner feedback score. The 3.74/4 feedback is self-reported.
